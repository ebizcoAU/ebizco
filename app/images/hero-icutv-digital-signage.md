## hero-icutv-digital-signage

**Context**: this replaces the homepage hero slide image for ICUTV. The previous image (`hero-icutv-addressable-tv.png`) depicted a living-room smart TV — that matched the old "addressable TV advertising" positioning, which was fictional (invented before the real ICUTV product content existed in this repo). ICUTV is actually a **pay-per-play digital signage network**: physical screens in public spaces (airports, shopping centres, buses, trains, taxis) managed from the cloud. The hero image needs to match that. As a stopgap the homepage currently uses the real `icutv-playanywhere.png` flat illustration (from the actual product's marketing kit) instead, but that's stylistically a flat illustration while the other three hero slides (OZLODGE, AntiFake, ProjMan) are moody photoreal photos — this prompt is for a photoreal replacement to match them.

**Size**: 1376×718px landscape (matches the other three hero images after their watermark crop). Rendered as a full-bleed hero background at `object-fit: cover` with a dark navy gradient scrim overlaid left-to-right (94% opaque on the left where white text sits, fading to 42% opaque on the right) — so, like the other three: keep the **left half darker/lower-contrast** since white heading text renders on top of it, and it's fine for the subject/detail to sit on the right half.

**Description**: A digital signage screen in a real public setting — e.g. a large commercial display mounted in an airport terminal, shopping centre concourse, or transit hub, playing a vivid ad/content. Moody, slightly dim ambient environment lighting (evening/dusk tone, matching the other three hero photos' dark cinematic look), with the screen itself as a bright focal point on the right side of the frame. Should read as "a real screen in the real world," not a studio product shot.

**Explicit exclusions**: no home/living-room setting (that was the old, incorrect concept), no readable brand logos or real company ads on the screen content (use abstract/generic colorful graphics instead), no people's faces in close-up, no text overlays baked into the image.
